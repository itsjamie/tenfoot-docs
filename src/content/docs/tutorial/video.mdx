## What you wrote

Every TV plays video the same way. The TV decodes the video into a layer under the page. The page draws on top of it. So your app owns the `<video>` element. You connect your player to it (Shaka, hls.js, or the TV's own player). Then you hand the element to `createRenderer` as `video`.

The renderer reads the video's clock and state. It gives you two things back:

- `r.controller`: play, pause, seek, `time()`, `state`, and an `on` function for changes.
- `r.subtitles`: a node that draws captions.

The controls are normal nodes. A dark strip, a title, and a progress bar that compares `controller.time()` to `state.duration`. When the state changes, `controller.on` asks for a redraw. While the video plays, a "dirty source" asks for a redraw four times a second. Nothing else runs. A paused player draws nothing.

Captions come from the `<track>` on the video element. The renderer picks up its cues. The `Subtitles` node shows them at the right time and lays them out. On a real TV, it uses the TV's own caption settings. `setAvoid` tells it which areas to stay out of. That is how captions move above your progress bar instead of under it.

## What happened

Click the picture and press <kbd>OK</kbd>. The status line shows the state, the time, and the current caption. Left and right jump ten seconds. Frames are drawn while the video plays, and stop when it stops.

## On a TV

The renderer tells `platform.keepAwake` about every play and pause. So the screensaver never comes on over a playing video. Samsung requires this, and the TV does not figure it out on its own.

The clip here is muted, because a web page cannot autoplay with sound. On a TV, the viewer pressed Play. Both LG and Samsung also expect Back during playback to stop the video and go back. The transport keys (`play`, `pause`, `rewind`, `fastForward`) arrive as actions once your app asks for them.

That is the end of the tutorial. [The reference app](../../reference-app/) uses these same eight ideas to build a full service. The [reference](../../reference/core/) lists every function, tier by tier.
